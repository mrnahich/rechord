Why we built ReChord

Think of a place that still means something to you. A bench where you used to sit after school. The corner where you heard the news. The platform where you said goodbye. You can probably picture it right now. What you can't do is go back and hear it.

That small, stubborn gap is where ReChord started.

Feeds forget

We share more than any generation before us. Photos, stories, captions, threads. And almost all of it has the same fate: it lands at the top of a timeline, gets a few seconds of attention, and slides down until nobody sees it again. A feed is built to move. That's what makes it good for news and bad for memory.

The other thing a feed does is cut a moment off from where it happened. A photo of a sunset might carry a location tag, but you will never come across it by standing in that spot. The physical world, where life actually happens, carries almost none of what we say about it.

Places don't

Places are the opposite of feeds. They stay. The bench is still there. The café still opens at eight. The platform still hums when the train comes in. Places are where our memories are anchored, even when nothing we've shared is anchored to them.

So we asked a simple question: what if a moment could stay exactly where it happened, and anyone who walked by later could hear it?

Why voice

We tried to picture the most honest way to leave something behind, and kept coming back to the human voice. A voice carries things text flattens: a laugh in the middle of a sentence, a pause before the hard part, the wind, the traffic, the music from a window upstairs. It's quick to make, there's nothing to edit, and it's hard to fake. Hearing a stranger's voice in the place they stood feels less like reading a post and more like meeting someone.

That's why ReChord is voice-first. No text posts, no filters. Just up to a minute of you, in your own voice.

What ReChord is

ReChord is a map of voices. You record a moment, and it's pinned to the spot where you're standing. You choose whether it shows at the exact spot or just nearby, and whether it's public or only for you. Then you, and anyone exploring the map, can find it, listen, and feel a little of what you felt there.

There's no timeline for a voice to sink in. It doesn't expire. A voice left today on a corner in Tehran, a beach in Brighton or a station in Zürich can be heard there next week, next year, or whenever someone wanders by.

The people behind it

ReChord started unexpectedly, as many meaningful things do: five people and one idea that wouldn't leave us alone. We come from engineering, business, marketing and product, and we've all had that moment of standing somewhere and wishing it could talk back. You can meet us on the Team page, where, fittingly, each of us left a voice on a little map.

Where we're going

ReChord is live on iPhone today and free on the App Store. We announce new things only when you can actually use them, and you can follow each release on our Updates page.

For now, our ask is small. Next time you're somewhere that matters, say something. Leave it there. Someone will find it.
