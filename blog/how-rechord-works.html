How ReChord works: a complete guide

ReChord does one thing: it lets you leave your voice in a place. This guide walks through everything the iPhone app does today, from your first recording to finding voices on the other side of the world.

1. Getting started

Download ReChord free from the App Store and create an account with your email, or sign in with Apple or Google. Pick a username and a display name, and add a profile photo if you like. That's all you need to start.

ReChord asks for two permissions, and it helps to know why:

  • Microphone, so you can record.
  • Location while you use the app, so a voice can be pinned where you are and the map can open around you. ReChord does not track your location in the background.

2. Recording a voice

Tap the microphone in the middle of the tab bar and start talking. A voice can be up to 60 seconds long. That's enough for a moment, a thought, a story, or a small "I was here." It's deliberately not enough to perform.

There are no filters and no editing. Background sound is part of it: the rain, the café, the waves, the band down the street. That's often what makes a voice worth hearing later.

3. Pinning it to a place

When you finish, your voice is pinned to the spot where you're standing. You choose how precise that is:

  • Precise pins the very bench, doorway or table.
  • Nearby shows it in the right neighbourhood, without the exact spot.

A good rule: use precise for public places, and nearby for anything close to your home, work or daily routine.

4. Public or private

Every voice is either public or private, and you decide with one switch before you send it.

  • A public voice appears on the map for everyone, along with your name, photo and the title you gave it.
  • A private voice is visible only to you. It's a way to keep a voice diary that lives on a map: where you were when you got the job, the street you walked on your last night in a city.

5. A title and a cover

Give your voice a short title and, if you like, a cover photo, so the place has a face. Name the place too, so listeners know where they are. A title like "Rain, and a piano upstairs" tells someone what they're about to hear before they press play.

6. Exploring the map

The map is the feed. Open it and you'll see voices around you as pins. Zoom into a street and listen to what happened there, or travel anywhere on Earth and explore a city you miss or have never seen. Tap a pin to play it. There's no algorithm deciding what's at the top: voices live where they were left.

7. Sharing a voice

Every public voice can be shared with one link. Send it in any chat, and it opens the voice right where it lives on the map, so there are no screenshots and no explaining where it was. People you send it to may be able to listen even before they install ReChord.

8. Likes, listens and activity

When someone listens to or likes your voice, it comes back to you. Your activity tab shows who loved what you left behind and where, and with notifications on, you'll know when it happens.

9. Staying safe

  • Think before you publish a precise location that reveals where you live, work or study.
  • Only record other people when they're happy to be recorded.
  • If you hear something that shouldn't be there, report it from the app. You can also block any user. They won't be told.
  • You can delete a voice, or your whole account, at any time from the app.

Your first voice

The best way to understand ReChord is to leave one voice. It doesn't need to be profound. Stand somewhere you like, press the button, and say why. Then go back next month and listen.
